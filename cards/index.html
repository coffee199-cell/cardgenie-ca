Canadian Credit Card Reviews

A review should tell you who a card is wrong for, not just who it is right for. These pages work through the published earn rates, the caps that limit them, the annual fee and the eligibility rules, then describe the spender who should skip the card entirely.

Research checked August 29, 2026

CardGenie tracks 40 active Canadian credit cards across 15 issuers in a database that records earn rates, category caps, annual fees, interest rates, insurance coverage and welcome offers separately, so a change to any one of them is visible. Reviews are written from that data rather than from issuer marketing copy.

If you want the answer for your own spending instead of a general verdict, the CardGenie calculator applies every card in the database to your monthly budget and ranks them by net annual value after the annual fee.

American Express Cobalt Card review

CA$155.88 per year, billed as CA$12.99 monthly. Earns 5x Membership Rewards on eligible groceries, restaurants, bars and food delivery up to CA$2,500 of spending each month, 3x on streaming, 2x on transit and travel, 1x on everything else. The strongest everyday earn rate in Canada if your food spending fits inside the cap.

Scotiabank Gold American Express review

CA$120 per year with no foreign transaction fee, which is unusual at this price. Earns 5x Scene+ on groceries, dining and entertainment with no monthly cap, 3x on gas, transit and streaming, 1x elsewhere. The uncapped grocery rate is what separates it from the Cobalt for larger households.

What every review checks

The earn rate on each category, and the cap that rate stops at. Caps are published inconsistently across issuers: some are monthly, some are annual, some are shared across several categories so that spending in one uses up the allowance for another. A shared cap is easy to miss and is the most frequent cause of a card earning less than a shopper calculated.

The annual fee, whether it is rebated in the first year, and whether supplementary cards cost extra. The purchase interest rate and cash advance rate, because a carried balance overwhelms any rewards rate within a few months. The minimum income requirement, which gates most Visa Infinite and World Elite Mastercard products. The insurance package, which is often the real reason to hold a premium travel card.

How to use a review

Read the cap section first, then check the card is accepted where you actually shop, then subtract the annual fee from your projected rewards. If the card still wins after all three, the welcome offer is a bonus rather than the reason to apply.

Reviews or comparisons

A review is the right format when you are evaluating a single card you already have in mind, usually because an offer prompted you or a bank suggested it. A head-to-head comparison is better when you have narrowed the field to two candidates. Category rankings under best cards are the right starting point when you do not yet have a shortlist at all.

The Short Version

No card is good or bad in isolation. A card is good when its uncapped categories match where your money goes, its annual fee is smaller than the extra rewards it produces, and it is accepted at the stores you visit weekly. Verify the current terms on the issuer's site before applying, because published rates and offers change without notice.
