Amex Cobalt vs Scotiabank Gold American Express

These two cards look almost identical in the advertising. Both are American Express products, both pay 5x on groceries and dining, both sit in the CA$120 to CA$156 annual fee range. The difference that decides it is a single number in the fine print: the Cobalt stops paying 5x after CA$2,500 of eligible spending each month, and the Scotiabank Gold does not stop.

Research checked August 29, 2026

The quick answer

If your household spends less than CA$2,500 a month combined on groceries, restaurants, bars and food delivery, both cards earn the same number of points on that spending. The Scotiabank Gold then wins on cost alone, because its annual fee is CA$120 against the Cobalt's CA$155.88, and it adds no foreign transaction fee on top.

If your household spends more than CA$2,500 a month on food and drink, the Scotiabank Gold wins by a wider margin, because the Cobalt drops to 1x on everything above the cap while the Scotiabank card keeps paying 5x.

The Cobalt's case rests entirely on Membership Rewards being worth more than 1 cent per point to you. If you transfer points to Aeroplan and redeem them well, that assumption can hold. If you redeem for statement credits, it does not.

Verdict

For most Canadian households, the Scotiabank Gold American Express is the better card: lower fee, no cap on the 5x rate, and no foreign transaction fee. Choose the Amex Cobalt if you actively transfer Membership Rewards to airline partners, or if you value the monthly billing structure, which spreads the fee across twelve payments of CA$12.99 instead of one annual charge.

What the difference is worth

All dollar figures on this page use CardGenie's standard point valuations: cash back at face value, Aeroplan at 1.5 cents per point, Membership Rewards and Scene+ at 1.0 cent per point. These are deliberately conservative. Points transferred to airline partners at the right time can be worth considerably more, and points redeemed for merchandise are usually worth less. The CardGenie calculator uses the same valuations, so it will not contradict the numbers here.

Take a household spending CA$1,000 a month on groceries and CA$400 on restaurants, so CA$1,400 of eligible food and drink. That sits comfortably under the Cobalt cap, so both cards earn 7,000 points a month, or 84,000 points a year, worth CA$840 at 1 cent. After the annual fee, the Cobalt nets CA$684 and the Scotiabank Gold nets CA$720. The Scotiabank card is ahead by CA$36, and that gap is purely the annual fee difference.

Now take a larger household spending CA$3,000 a month on food and drink. The Cobalt pays 5x on the first CA$2,500 and 1x on the last CA$500, earning 13,000 points a month. The Scotiabank Gold pays 5x on all of it, earning 15,000 points a month. Over a year that is CA$1,404 net for the Cobalt against CA$1,680 net for the Scotiabank Gold, a gap of CA$276.

The pattern is consistent: at low food spending the two cards tie on points and the fee decides it, and at high food spending the cap decides it. There is no spending level at which the Cobalt wins on cash-equivalent value alone.

Choose the Scotiabank Gold if

  • Your combined grocery, restaurant and bar spending exceeds CA$2,500 in some months
  • You spend in foreign currencies, online or while travelling
  • You buy movie tickets, concerts or streaming and want the 5x entertainment rate
  • You fill up regularly and want 3x on gas rather than 1x
  • You want predictable value and would redeem points as travel or a statement credit

Choose the Amex Cobalt if

  • You transfer Membership Rewards to Aeroplan or other partners and redeem above 1 cent per point
  • Your food and drink spending fits inside CA$2,500 a month and you value points flexibility over the CA$36 fee difference
  • You prefer a CA$12.99 monthly charge to a single annual fee
  • You want 2x on travel booked directly rather than 1x

The acceptance problem applies to both

Both cards run on the American Express network, so both share the same weakness. Amex is accepted at most large Canadian grocery chains and national retailers, but refusals remain common at independent grocers, smaller restaurants, some gas stations and many local businesses. A 5x rate at a store that will not take the card is worth nothing.

Before choosing either card, check the three or four merchants where most of your grocery and restaurant spending actually happens. If a meaningful share of that spending goes somewhere Amex is refused, a Visa or Mastercard with a lower published rate may earn you more in practice. That is a case for comparing against a card like the Scotiabank Momentum or TD Cash Back Visa Infinite instead.

The Short Version

Same network, same headline rate, different fine print. The Scotiabank Gold American Express charges less, does not cap the 5x rate, and drops the foreign transaction fee, which makes it the stronger card for most households on conservative point values. The Amex Cobalt is the better choice for people who will genuinely work the Membership Rewards transfer partners, and a worse one for people who will not.

Common questions

Does the Amex Cobalt 5x cap reset every month?

Yes. The Cobalt pays 5x Membership Rewards on eligible eats and drinks purchases up to CA$2,500 of spending in each monthly billing cycle, then 1x above that. The allowance resets with each statement rather than accumulating, so a month where you spend CA$1,000 does not give you extra room the following month.

Is groceries and dining one shared cap on the Cobalt or two separate caps?

One shared cap. Groceries, restaurants, bars and food delivery all draw from the same CA$2,500 monthly allowance. This is the single most misread detail on the card. A household spending CA$1,800 on groceries and CA$900 on restaurants has already passed the cap and is earning 1x on the last CA$200.

Which card is better for foreign currency spending?

The Scotiabank Gold American Express, clearly. It charges no foreign transaction fee, which removes the usual 2.5 percent surcharge on purchases billed in a foreign currency. The Cobalt charges the standard 2.5 percent. On CA$4,000 of foreign spending a year that difference alone is CA$100, which is most of the gap between the two annual fees.

Can I hold both cards at the same time?

Yes, and some people do, because the two cards earn into different programs. The usual arrangement is to put the first CA$2,500 of monthly food and drink spending on the Cobalt and route the overflow to the Scotiabank Gold rather than earning 1x. Whether that is worth two annual fees depends on how far past the cap your household spends.

Are Membership Rewards points worth more than Scene+ points?

They can be. Scene+ points redeem at a predictable 1 cent each against travel or as a statement credit against eligible purchases. Membership Rewards points also redeem near 1 cent for statement credits, but they can transfer to Aeroplan and other airline partners, where a well-timed redemption can exceed that. That upside is real but it is not automatic, and it requires you to actually learn a loyalty program.
